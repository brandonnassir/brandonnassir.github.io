Analyzing Embedding Representations of SAT Problems

This project investigates how well semantic embeddings of SAT Math problems—and their related forms such as problem summaries and solutions—reflect known labels like skill, domain, problem type, and difficulty. The goal was to assess whether language models can internally distinguish between these categories based on the embeddings they generate.

01

Skill and Domain Are Strongly Encoded

The models show a strong internal representation of what kind of fine grained skills are being tested. Problems with the same domain and skills are closer together.

02

Difficulty Is Less Well Captured

Problem embeddings show only moderate clustering by difficulty. This makes sense, difficulty is a more abstract, psychometric property. Two problems requiring the same skill can vary in cognitive demand, which may not be visible in the text.

03

Different Representations Offer Complementary Insights

Solutions expose which operations or procedures are used, improving skill identification. Summaries distill the intent of the problem author and key concepts, helping models capture instructional meaning more precisely.

04

Implications for Model Training

If embeddings were reorganized to better reflect difficulty and intent, it could support adaptive assessment systems, targeted content generation, and automated quality control pipelines for educational content.

Conclusion

Embedding spaces derived from modern language models encode significant semantic structure. While skill and domain information is strongly captured, difficulty remains a challenging attribute to encode.

With further tuning, these embeddings could power sophisticated applications in problem generation, curriculum alignment, and automated difficulty calibration—ultimately reducing manual effort in cirriculum development.
